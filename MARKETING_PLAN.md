# ÉclaireSkin audience plan

## Positioning

ÉclaireSkin is for people who feel overwhelmed by skincare advice and want a simple, independent place to understand their skin. Lead with the outcome — clarity, confidence, and returning gently to self-care — rather than with AI.

Primary starting audience: New Zealand women aged roughly 28–45 who are rebuilding a routine after pregnancy, stress, hormonal change, or a long break. This is narrow enough to reach and closely matches the founder story.

Core message: **“Understand what your skin needs before buying another product.”**

## Legal gate before public acquisition

ÉclaireSkin should not run paid campaigns or actively promote health-related content until New Zealand counsel has reviewed the product scope and the reusable content format. The current New Zealand Therapeutic and Health Advertising Code applies to health advertising on an advertiser's own website, social channels, apps, and email as well as paid media.

Work can continue safely as preparation rather than publication:

1. Draft the article template, author page, sourcing standard, disclaimer, and first content cluster.
2. Give counsel one representative article, the page CTA, the product description, and the AI-analysis flow to review together.
3. Turn counsel's feedback into a written claims checklist used for every future page.
4. Publish only pages that pass that checklist; route new claim types back for review.

Avoid therapeutic promises, diagnosis, prevention/treatment language, implied professional endorsement, guaranteed outcomes, and before/after claims until counsel has expressly approved the relevant wording and evidence standard. A disclaimer does not repair an otherwise misleading or therapeutic claim.

## Channel roles

### Search: the acquisition engine

Publish useful pages for questions people already ask. Begin with:

1. Dehydrated skin vs dry skin
2. How to start retinol with sensitive skin
3. A simple skincare routine after pregnancy
4. Niacinamide for sensitive or dehydrated skin
5. Damaged skin barrier: signs and a gentle routine

Each page should answer the question fully, use cautious educational language, include a short disclaimer, and end with one soft invitation to the free skin profile.

Use a small, high-quality topic cluster instead of mass-producing AI articles. Each page should include:

- a clear byline and honest explanation of how the article was created and reviewed
- primary or reputable clinical sources linked near the relevant claims
- a last-reviewed date that changes only after a substantive review
- plain-English distinctions between appearance, common skincare concerns, and conditions requiring professional assessment
- an educational disclaimer and a non-diagnostic CTA
- a descriptive title, meta description, canonical URL, internal links, and inclusion in the XML sitemap

Google treats health-adjacent topics as especially trust-sensitive. The goal is therefore not maximum article volume; it is a small library that is genuinely useful, original, transparent about authorship and AI assistance, and strong enough that a reader would bookmark or share it.

### Instagram: trust and proof

Use the founder account and first-person storytelling. Publish three strong pieces per week rather than posting every day:

- One founder/story Reel: the real situation that caused the skincare question.
- One educational carousel: a useful distinction or framework worth saving.
- One product demonstration: show the questions, the result, and what changed without presenting the result as a diagnosis.

Every post should have one job: reach, save, reply, or click. Avoid generic ingredient facts unless they connect to a real problem.

### Email: retention

The welcome sequence should help before it sells:

1. Immediately: welcome, brand promise, and a link back to the skin profile.
2. Day 2: “Dry or dehydrated?” with a practical self-check.
3. Day 5: the three-step minimum routine.
4. Day 9: founder story and a request for one reply about their biggest frustration.

### Partnerships: borrowed trust

Approach small, aligned New Zealand audiences rather than skincare brands: postpartum educators, women’s wellbeing newsletters, beauty therapists, pharmacists, and micro-creators who discuss realistic self-care. Offer a useful co-created guide or free audience workshop, not a promotional post.

### Reddit: research and reputation only

Do not post the app, evade bans, create alternate accounts, or place promotional links in comments. Read public discussions to learn the audience’s language and recurring questions. Participate only where the community rules permit it and only to give a complete, useful answer with no commercial mention. Treat traffic from Reddit as a possible side effect, not a target.

## First 30 days

### Week 1 — measurement and message

- Finish analytics and the email welcome sequence.
- Interview five target users for 20 minutes each.
- Record their exact language for confusion, frustration, and desired outcomes.
- Create one landing-page promise for the chosen starting audience.

### Week 2 — first search cluster

- Publish the dehydrated-vs-dry cornerstone page.
- Publish two supporting pages answering narrower questions.
- Turn the cornerstone into one Reel, one carousel, and one email.

### Week 3 — founder proof

- Publish the founder story with the specific before/after understanding, not exaggerated skin results.
- Share three anonymised examples of what users learned.
- Contact ten highly relevant small partners with personalised, non-spam outreach.

### Week 4 — learn and repeat

- Compare search impressions, profile starts, profile completions, and email captures.
- Interview users who completed the profile and users who abandoned it.
- Keep the best-performing problem/message pair and build the next content cluster around it.

## Weekly scorecard

- Qualified visits by channel
- Skin-profile start rate
- Skin-profile completion rate
- First analysis or routine completion rate
- Email capture rate
- Seven-day return rate
- Search impressions and clicks by query
- Email replies and user-interview learnings

Follower count and raw page views are secondary. The useful signal is whether the right person completes the skin profile and returns.
