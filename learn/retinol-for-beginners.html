Retinol for beginners: start gently

Retinol is popular for a reason, but it is not a race. Understanding the retinoid family—and your own context—matters more than chasing the strongest percentage.

ÉclaireSkin editorial teamIngredient guideLast reviewed: not yet reviewed

What is retinol?

Retinol is a vitamin A derivative and one member of the broader retinoid family. Cosmetic retinol is different from prescription retinoids such as tretinoin. Product strength cannot be compared by percentage alone because formulation and the specific retinoid both affect how a product behaves.

Retinoids are used in dermatology and cosmetic skincare for different purposes. DermNet notes that topical retinoids may also cause dryness, peeling, irritation and sun sensitivity. That is why a gradual, label-led approach is sensible.

Questions to ask before starting

  • Is your skin currently comfortable, or already stinging and peeling?
  • Are you pregnant, trying to conceive or breastfeeding?
  • Are you using prescription acne treatment or another exfoliating active?
  • Can you use sun protection consistently?
  • Does the product label give clear instructions and warnings?
Pregnancy matters: some topical retinoids are contraindicated during pregnancy. Ask a qualified healthcare professional about your specific circumstances rather than relying on a general article or app.

A cautious introduction

  1. Follow the exact label or prescriber instructions.
  2. Patch test as directed.
  3. Begin with a simple cleanser–moisturiser–sunscreen foundation.
  4. Introduce retinol without adding several other new actives at the same time.
  5. Avoid the eye corners, lips and broken or irritated skin unless the product guidance says otherwise.
  6. Use daytime sun protection and respond early if irritation builds.

What if your skin becomes irritated?

Mild dryness can occur, but persistent burning, marked redness, swelling, blistering or worsening discomfort is a reason to stop and seek professional advice. DermNet notes that topical retinoids can worsen eczema because of their drying effect.

Do not assume that irritation proves a product is “working.” A routine should be tolerable enough to use consistently.

Retinol is optional

You can have a thoughtful skincare routine without retinol. A gentle cleanser, suitable moisturiser and sun protection remain useful foundations. Your goals, tolerance, life stage and professional advice should decide whether a retinoid belongs in your routine.
